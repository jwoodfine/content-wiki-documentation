Corporate news releases carry more scrutiny than ordinary wiki prose: they are read by press, by regulators, and by counterparties evaluating a disclosure's credibility at a glance. A release that reads inconsistently across formats — a jagged justified-text block here, an all-caps headline there — reads as less institutionally credible regardless of its content. The platform's news-release format follows ISO 24495-1 Plain Language principles for exactly this reason: consistency of layout is itself part of what makes a [[disclosure-substrate|disclosure]] read as authoritative, across both digital and physical mediums.

## Universal layout

Every release is left-aligned (ragged right) rather than justified — justified text produces erratic word spacing that disrupts reading flow at the density a release demands. Headlines and corporate entity names use Title Case; all-caps blocks are avoided for the same clarity reason. Provinces and states are spelled out in full ("British Columbia" rather than "BC"), removing any ambiguity for an international reader unfamiliar with a jurisdiction's postal abbreviations.

## Print presentation

A physical release is set with a bold, Title Case header and a dateline separated from the narrative by an en-dash or em-dash, following a standard "For Immediate Release" structure — headline, then a Vancouver, British Columbia dateline, then the narrative body. This is the print convention institutional readers already expect from a press release, which is why the platform follows it rather than inventing its own.

## Digital presentation

A web-based release uses an H2 for the release category (for example, "Operational Update") and an H1 for the headline, with a pipe symbol separating the dateline elements — corporate entity and release category, then headline, then a "For immediate release" line with location and date — on their own metadata lines. The semantic-heading structure gives a screen reader and a search engine the same hierarchy a print reader gets from typography alone.

Together these three layers keep every platform disclosure reading with the same [[editorial-language-registers|voice of authority]], whether viewed on a high-DPI display or a physical printed page.
